# Act 1 Story Outline: Last Line

## Purpose

Act 1 is the story of a train that begins as an escape vehicle and becomes a promise to return for others.

The emotional spine is:

1. Survive.
2. Hear the first signal.
3. Choose to help strangers.
4. See the raider philosophy.
5. Defend the train from inside.
6. Inherit the last message.
7. Stop the engine that runs on plunder.

The player is not proving that they are a hero. They are proving that even after collapse, a different way to live can still be chosen.

## Story Pillars

- Survival first, heroism later.
- The train is a community, not a vehicle skin.
- Raiders are not random bandits. They are the Act 1 argument against Last Rail's humanism.
- Rewards should feel like repaired trust, rescued capacity, or recovered future, not loot from conquest.
- The first signal should begin as hope, become danger, and end as inheritance.

## Primary Cast Roles

### Commander

The player character. The Commander can be quiet, but their identity is expressed through route choices, stops, and who the train refuses to abandon.

### Conductor

The practical voice of the train. Starts as a survival-first realist: "We cannot stop for every light in the dark." By the end of Act 1, the Conductor is the first to say, "We stop."

### Signal Officer

Believes the first transmission matters before there is proof. Carries the mystery thread and interprets damaged messages.

### Chief Mechanic

Treats the train as a living body. Grounds the story in material limits: water, couplers, heat, brakes, spare steel.

### Raider King

Not just a person, but a title and a machine-order. The Raider King represents a system that turns fear into fuel. Suggested names: Grave Engine, Crown Engine, Iron King.

## Mission Beats

### opn_01_last_departure: Last Departure

#### Premise

The story opens in a sealed maintenance station during its final evacuation window. The train is unfinished, undermanned, and carrying people who do not yet trust each other. The Commander is given field authority because no one else can coordinate the defense and departure at the same time.

#### Story Goal

Leave before the station doors fail.

#### Combat Goal

Teach basic defense by protecting the engine startup sequence, power relays, and one passenger carriage. Victory is departure, not annihilation.

#### Key Dramatic Turn

The train cannot take everyone unless the Commander holds the platform longer than planned. The first act of command is not speed. It is buying seconds for more people to board.

#### Sample Lines

- Conductor: "Commander, the timetable ended with the world. Give me an order that gets us moving."
- Chief Mechanic: "The engine will run. It will complain, curse, and try to die, but it will run."
- Passenger, after boarding: "You waited. Nobody waits anymore."

#### Unlock / Reward Feeling

Basic train systems, first operators, and first repeat rewards should feel like emergency supplies recovered from the station, not treasure.

### opn_02_first_signal: First Signal

#### Premise

After departure, the train catches a repeating rescue signal from an old communications mast. It may be a survivor, an automated loop, or a trap. The Conductor argues for conserving fuel. The Signal Officer argues that if the train ignores the first voice it hears, it has already decided what kind of world it accepts.

#### Story Goal

Restore enough of the mast to decode the message.

#### Combat Goal

Protect signal equipment while the transmission is reconstructed. Enemy waves should feel like interference and pressure rather than a clean battlefield.

#### Key Dramatic Turn

The message resolves only partly: "Do not let the King's Engine reach the broken line." The signal gives direction, but not comfort.

#### Sample Lines

- Signal Officer: "It is not a full message. It is a hand under rubble."
- Conductor: "A signal can be bait."
- Commander response prompt candidate: "Then we spring the trap carefully."

#### Unlock / Reward Feeling

Communication and scouting features can be framed as recovered signal maps, antenna modules, or route intelligence.

### opn_03_water_stop: Water Stop

#### Premise

The train reaches a water station controlled by frightened survivors. They are not villains; they are people forced into ration math. A raider cell has been using access to water to decide who is allowed to live.

#### Story Goal

Restart the pumps without turning the station into a battlefield full of civilians.

#### Combat Goal

Defend pump machinery and protect civilian zones. Optional objectives can reward preserving infrastructure or preventing collateral damage.

#### Key Dramatic Turn

The Commander chooses to trade protection and repair work for water instead of seizing it. This is the first public proof that Last Rail's way can work.

#### Sample Lines

- Station Elder: "If you came to take the water, take a number. Everyone does."
- Chief Mechanic: "Pumps do not care who owns them. They care who knows how to listen."
- Conductor, after success: "We spent ammunition to leave with permission. I did not expect that to feel efficient."

#### Unlock / Reward Feeling

Machine parts, credits, and operator EXP rewards fit here as repair payment and shared supplies.

### opn_04_scrapstar_show: Scrapstar Show

#### Premise

The train enters a scrap market built around spectacle and coercion. The Raiders broadcast fights, auctions, and public humiliations to turn fear into entertainment. They announce the Last Rail as the next featured prize.

#### Story Goal

Break the broadcast, rescue captives, and expose the Raiders' system.

#### Combat Goal

Fight through arena-like hazards and broadcast towers. Introduce enemy modifiers or environmental gimmicks that make the player feel watched and pressured.

#### Key Dramatic Turn

The Raiders are not merely stealing supplies. They are selling the idea that mercy is obsolete. The Commander counters by saving prisoners during a fight that would be easier if they ignored them.

#### Sample Lines

- Raider Announcer: "Look at that. A train full of soft hearts and warm fuel."
- Signal Officer: "They are not broadcasting victory. They are broadcasting permission."
- Conductor: "Then let us interrupt the program."

#### Unlock / Reward Feeling

Gacha or recruitment narrative can be seeded here carefully: rescued specialists, defectors, or people inspired by the train. Avoid making recruitment feel like buying people.

### opn_05_night_boarders: Night Boarders

#### Premise

The Raiders stop attacking the route and board the train at night. The threat moves inside the community. Lights fail, carriages lock down, and suspicion spreads among passengers.

#### Story Goal

Retake the train without breaking trust inside it.

#### Combat Goal

Defend multiple carriage zones, recover key systems, and prevent boarders from reaching the engine or infirmary. This mission should feel claustrophobic compared with prior route defense.

#### Key Dramatic Turn

Passengers who were previously background civilians help hold doors, carry ammunition, or guide children through maintenance crawlspaces. The train becomes a community that defends itself.

#### Sample Lines

- Passenger Militia Volunteer: "I am not trained."
- Commander prompt candidate: "Stand where you can be brave and still survive."
- Conductor: "They came for a train. They found a city with wheels."

#### Unlock / Reward Feeling

Risk tiers or higher difficulty can be narratively framed here: the train can choose safer routes, but harder routes protect more people or recover more critical supplies.

### opn_06_broken_line: Broken Line

#### Premise

The signal source is beyond a collapsed bridge and a torn rail junction. The Raiders are massing because the same route leads to the King's Engine. The train must repair a temporary crossing under attack.

#### Story Goal

Recover the final message and reopen the broken route.

#### Combat Goal

Protect repair teams, hold bridge segments, and survive timed pressure while the line is reconnected. The final wave should feel like the raid's shadow arriving early.

#### Key Dramatic Turn

The source of the signal is not a living settlement. It is a final record from people who held the line long enough to warn the next train. The message asks the next crew not to repeat the old mistake: "Do not save the engine and abandon the people."

#### Sample Lines

- Signal Record: "If anyone hears this, the line is not dead. It is waiting for hands."
- Chief Mechanic: "A rail is just steel until someone decides where it should lead."
- Conductor: "Commander, the crossing will not hold forever."

#### Unlock / Reward Feeling

First-clear rewards should feel like inherited supplies: sealed caches, last-route maps, and preserved cores left by the fallen station crew.

## Act 1 Raid: raid_raider_king_engine

### Premise

The Raider King arrives as an armored command engine built from stolen cars, fuel tanks, and trophies. The raid target is not only the vehicle, but the belief that fear is the most efficient form of order.

### Recommended Boss Identity

Use "Raider King" as the public title. Internally and in dialogue, the engine can be called the Crown Engine. This lets the story distinguish the person, the machine, and the ideology.

### Raid Structure

#### Phase 1: Chase the Crown

The King's Engine tries to force Last Rail off the route. The player disables escort cars and survives route hazards.

#### Phase 2: Break the Trophies

The player targets attached cars that hold stolen supplies, prisoners, or broadcast equipment. Destroying everything is faster, but rescuing people or preserving supplies better fits the Act 1 theme.

#### Phase 3: Stop the Engine

The final phase focuses on disabling the command engine without detonating the fuel core. The Last Rail wins by stopping the machine and separating people from the Raider King's system.

### Ending Beat

The captured raider cars contain people who expected execution, not rescue. The Conductor opens the doors. The train becomes heavier, slower, and more worth defending.

### Sample Lines

- Raider King: "Mercy is weight. Weight slows a train."
- Conductor: "Add another carriage."
- Signal Officer: "The first signal is gone, Commander. But we are transmitting now."

## Character Arc Summary

### Commander

From emergency authority to chosen responsibility.

### Conductor

From survival calculus to principled stopping.

### Signal Officer

From faith in a weak signal to becoming the next signal.

### Chief Mechanic

From keeping the engine alive to deciding what the engine is for.

### Train Community

From passengers to crew.

## System Tie-ins

### Risk / Reward

Risk levels should be presented as route danger and rescue ambition, not just harder enemy stats. Higher risk can mean worse weather, more exposed track, greater civilian stakes, or richer but harder-to-secure salvage.

### First Clear Rewards

First clear rewards should represent trust, inheritance, or recovered one-time caches. This supports the server policy that first clear is a separate rewardLine.

### Gacha / Recruitment

Recruitment should be framed as people joining the Last Rail's cause, not ownership. Act 1 can seed recruits through rescued passengers, defectors, and operators responding to the train's growing signal.

### Ops Error / Diagnostics

Story-facing UI should never imply raw logs, tokens, or hidden probability. Admin/QA diagnostic language can refer to route, risk, mission, result, and reward plan status.

## Open Questions

- Does the Commander speak directly, or are their choices represented through prompt labels and NPC responses?
- Should the Raider King be a named human antagonist in Act 1, or should the "King" remain mostly a title attached to the Crown Engine?
- Which Act 1 operator is the player's emotional anchor: Conductor, Signal Officer, Chief Mechanic, or first recruited field operator?
- Does Act 1 end with a clear destination for Act 2, or only a stronger outgoing signal?
